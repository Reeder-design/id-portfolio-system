About Me

I design learning for complex products and the systems that support it

Most of my work sits where instructional design, technical content, and learning operations overlap. I build for sales and partner audiences, but I also work on the LMS, reporting, automation, AI evaluation, and custom content when those pieces affect the learner experience.

I started as a high school history and English teacher and earned a Master of Education before moving into corporate L&D. That background still shows up in how I design: clear objectives, useful practice, direct feedback, and a constant check on whether the experience helps someone perform better.

How I adapt the work to the learning need

I don't start with a tool or format. I start with who needs to do what, what makes that difficult, and what constraints shape the experience. The method changes by the work; the standard stays the same: make it useful, usable, and maintainable.

Instructional Design for eLearning

Design around the performance, then choose the format.

Sales, partner, technical, and internal audiences do not need the same amount of context, practice, or support. I adjust the depth, pacing, examples, and delivery to the audience and the job they need to do.

How I think about it

Start with the performance gap and the smallest learning solution that can close it. Objectives, practice, assessment, and support resources should all point to the same outcome.

How the work changes

I might use a full pathway for foundational knowledge, a scenario for judgment, multimedia for a process or concept, or a job aid when the need is retrieval rather than instruction.

What good looks like

Clear structure, relevant practice, accessible design, efficient development, clean review and QA, and source files or templates that another designer can maintain.
