Why AI Evaluation Is Different

AI output can sound polished and still miss the job.

AI evaluation is not a general impression of whether an answer is good. I first define the task, the evidence an output may use, the intended audience, and the one quality question a reviewer needs to answer.

That makes the standard useful: the same response can be clear but unsupported, accurate but incomplete, or well designed but hard to navigate. Each is a different problem with a different rubric.

Job

What the output must help someone do.

Evidence

What can support the response.

Lens

The one quality question in scope.

One model output. Two separate review jobs.

CONTENT REVIEW

Is the response right for its task?

Check the request, evidence boundary, logic, and practical usefulness.

Task fitGroundingUsefulness
PRESENTATION REVIEW

Can people use the experience?

Check hierarchy, legibility, navigation, consistency, and template fit.

HierarchyLegibilityFlow

The content lane follows the evidence. The presentation lane follows the learner or user experience. Both can matter, but they should not be scored as the same thing.

Human Evaluation + Training

Turn quality judgment into a repeatable practice.

Reliable programs make judgment teachable. Reviewers receive task context, observable criteria, examples, score anchors, and feedback patterns instead of a vague thumbs-up or thumbs-down instruction.

01 / FRAME Frame the job

Set the task, audience, allowed evidence, and exclusions before anyone scores an output.

How I contribute: I build the practical pieces that make this loop usable: review packets, scenario practice, evidence trails, rubric language, benchmark comparisons, and feedback that makes the reason for a decision clear. The public demos below use fictional, sanitized scenarios to show the approach without exposing private evaluation material.

From Observation to Decision

A defensible AI evaluation has five connected parts.

A lens defines the question. A rubric defines the standard. A quality signal is the evidence. The rationale explains why it matters, and calibration checks whether the judgment is consistent.
