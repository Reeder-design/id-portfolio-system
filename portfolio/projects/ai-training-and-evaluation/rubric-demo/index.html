Evaluate the output—not the polish.

Review a fictional AI-generated learning artifact against the actual task and approved source information. Then score the output, classify its overall severity, and compare your reasoning with an expert benchmark.

Step 1

Review the task and evidence.

A defensible evaluation requires knowing what the model was asked to do and what information it was actually allowed to use.

Original Task Create a five-minute microlearning lesson for newly hired sales representatives introducing the fictional product AtlasLink. The lesson must:
  • include one measurable learning objective;
  • explain three selling points supported by the approved source brief;
  • include one realistic customer scenario;
  • include a knowledge check that requires the learner to apply what they learned;
  • provide feedback explaining why the correct answer is appropriate.

Approved Source Brief

AtlasLink Product Information

  • AtlasLink is a fictional managed networking platform for organizations with distributed branch locations.
  • The strongest-fit customer has multiple locations and needs centralized management of branch connectivity.
  • AtlasLink allows administrators to manage branch network policies from a central interface.
  • Standardized policy templates can reduce configuration differences across locations.
  • AtlasLink supports automated failover between wired and cellular connectivity.
  • No approved percentage for cost reduction has been provided.
  • AtlasLink is not positioned as the best solution for every organization.
Evaluator reminder: A claim that sounds reasonable is not automatically grounded. Evaluate the draft against the information above—not against assumptions about what a networking product might be able to do.

AI-Generated Draft

Introducing AtlasLink

AtlasLink is a next-generation networking solution designed to help organizations modernize their business infrastructure.

Learning Objective

By the end of this lesson, learners will understand AtlasLink and why customers should buy it.

Three Key Selling Points

  • AtlasLink reduces network operating costs by up to 60%.
  • AtlasLink provides fast, reliable connectivity for modern organizations.
  • AtlasLink is easy to deploy and works for virtually every type of customer.

Customer Scenario

A customer tells you they want a better network. What should you recommend?

A. AtlasLink
B. Nothing
C. Tell the customer networking is not important

Correct answer: A

Feedback: Great job! AtlasLink is the best choice because it provides modern networking capabilities.

Instruction Adherence

Did the model follow the explicit directions, required format, and constraints?

Ask: What did the prompt explicitly require, and what is actually present?
Accuracy + Grounding

Are factual and product claims supported by the approved source information?

Ask: Can each substantive claim be traced to evidence provided in the task?
Completeness

Does the output include the information necessary to accomplish the requested purpose?

Ask: Is something technically present but substantively incomplete?
Reasoning + Consistency

Do the conclusions follow logically from the information provided?

Ask: Has the model reached a conclusion that the available evidence does not actually support?
Clarity + Usability

Can the intended user understand and navigate the output efficiently?

Ask: Is the artifact usable even if some of its underlying content needs revision?
Audience + Task Alignment

Does the output support what this specific audience needs to do in the real world?

Ask: Could a learner complete this lesson without being able to make the intended sales decision?

Expert Benchmark

Why this output needs major revision.

The benchmark is 60% with an overall severity classification of Major. The draft is readable and structurally complete, but several failures materially affect whether a new seller could rely on it.

Grounding Failure

The model invents a 60% cost-reduction claim that does not appear in the source. It also claims AtlasLink works for virtually every customer even though the source explicitly limits the ideal-customer profile.

Weak Learning Objective

“Understand AtlasLink” does not define observable performance. A stronger objective would ask the learner to identify an appropriate customer fit and explain relevant value propositions.

Invalid Application Practice

“The customer wants a better network” provides too little evidence to determine whether AtlasLink is appropriate. The distractors are also unrealistic, making the answer obvious.

Feedback Doesn't Teach

The feedback repeats that AtlasLink is “the best choice” without connecting the decision to customer characteristics or explaining why the other options are inappropriate.

Missing Strongest Evidence

The source provides concrete selling points—centralized management, standardized policies, and automated failover—but the generated lesson replaces them with vague claims.

What Is Worth Keeping

The draft is short, readable, and organized into useful sections. A good revision would preserve that structure while correcting the content and improving the instructional design.

Human Impact

Why classify this as a major issue?

A newly hired seller could leave this lesson believing that an unsupported 60% savings claim is approved messaging and that AtlasLink should be recommended to nearly any customer.

The artifact is not merely imperfect. It could create incorrect customer conversations and reinforce poor qualification behavior. That makes the grounding and application failures materially important.
