AI Training and Evaluation

Evaluation changes with the task.

AI evaluation is not one universal checklist. A spreadsheet, facilitator guide, assessment, slide deck, and business document may all require accuracy and instruction adherence—but the risks, failure modes, and human consequences can be very different.

Evaluation principle:

The goal is not to ask, “Do I like this output?” The goal is to determine whether the output satisfies the task, is supported by available evidence, works for the intended human audience, and can be trusted for the context in which it will actually be used.

Professional Evaluation Principles

What keeps the evaluation useful and defensible.

Evaluate Against Requirements

Personal writing preference is not the same as a quality failure. I anchor judgments to instructions, source evidence, rubric criteria, and intended use.

Don't Fill In Missing Context

If the output leaves out required information, I evaluate what is actually present rather than mentally repairing the response for the model.

Match Severity to Impact

A typo and a fabricated financial assumption are not equivalent. Severity should reflect how much an issue changes trust, usefulness, safety, or task success.

Consider the Human User

Quality depends partly on what a real person needs to do with the output. I consider discoverability, comprehension, maintenance, decision-making, and downstream consequences.

Make Feedback Diagnostic

Strong feedback identifies the issue, explains why it matters, and describes what a better result should do differently.

QA the Evaluation Too

The evaluator can introduce inconsistency. I recheck whether the score, severity, written rationale, and evidence all agree before finalizing the review.
