01

Need

What needed to change?

Create a consistent and maintainable way for internal and channel sellers with different levels of prior knowledge to understand a complex enterprise technology portfolio, recognize suitable opportunities, communicate value, navigate regional requirements, and prepare for the next stage of a sale.

Audience

Internal sellers and channel partners, from newcomers to experienced practitioners, working with a complex enterprise technology portfolio.

What learners needed to do

  • Recognize customer opportunities that fit the portfolio and identify the most relevant solution path.
  • Distinguish related solution categories using customer needs, constraints, and business context.
  • Ask useful discovery questions and connect technical capabilities to customer value without drifting into implementation training.
  • Recommend appropriate next steps and recognize when a specialist or technical handoff is needed.
  • Demonstrate required knowledge through objective-aligned assessment and remediation.
02

Design Decisions

How I shaped the solution

Approach

I treated the certification as a connected learning system rather than a single course. I organized the experience as a progressive journey, separated broadly applicable and regional requirements, layered content for mixed experience levels, tied technical detail to seller decisions, and aligned practice, assessment, LMS behavior, reporting, and maintenance to the same learner outcomes.

05

Audience Strategy

Serve novices and experienced sellers without designing two separate programs.

The audience ranged from people new to the portfolio to experienced sellers and partners. I used layering and modularity rather than forcing one level of detail on everyone.

Scaffold

Newer sellers

Use plain-language scaffolds, predictable sequencing, concise takeaways, and enough context to build a shared mental model before asking for application.

Layer

Experienced sellers

Provide expandable detail, quick-reference elements, and optional depth so experienced learners can move efficiently without losing access to useful context.

Focus

Role boundary

Keep technical detail only when it supports a seller decision, customer question, risk discussion, ordering step, or specialist handoff. Route deeper execution content to separate technical learning.

07

Assessment Strategy

Use assessment as part of the pathway, not a final attachment.

Formative checks corrected misconceptions during learning, while final assessment and remediation stayed tied to course objectives. A separate regional assessment closed a measurement gap without duplicating the entire path.

Practice

Formative checks

Use feedback during the course to correct misunderstandings before they reach the final assessment.

Validate

Primary assessment

Map final items to broadly required objectives and source courses so the certification reflects the learning path rather than isolated trivia.

Route

Regional assessment

Keep market-specific requirements in a supplemental assessment rather than forcing irrelevant required learning into the core pathway.

Recover

Remediation

Point learners back to the relevant topic when an answer reveals a specific misconception or missing requirement.

13

Reflection

What this project changed in how I design learning systems.

The work reinforced that curriculum design, governance, assessment, reporting, and LMS behavior cannot be treated as separate workstreams when learners experience them as one system.

Lesson

What I learned

A strong learning design has to survive the platform and maintenance realities around it. I also learned to draw a sharper boundary between technical knowledge needed for a seller decision and technical knowledge that belongs in a separate role-based path.

Next

What I would improve

I would define routing and reporting rules earlier, establish a source-of-truth inventory before development, prototype certification logic with representative learner profiles, and move more assessment toward fictional performance scenarios.

14

Outcome

What came out of the work?

  • Designed and developed a multi-course sales enablement pathway with role-based content, interactive practice, assessments, review documentation, and LMS implementation support.
  • The pathway was released and later restructured to provide distinct broadly applicable and regional learning routes.
  • A supplemental regional assessment and automated spreadsheet workflow provided a practical way to classify regional completion states when platform reporting did not fully match the learning design.
  • Project records support staged QA, stakeholder review, issue resolution, maintenance, and migration validation without relying on unsupported performance or revenue claims.
