Cellular Networking Sales Certification

I rebuilt a multi-course sales certification as two business operations and product lines came together, a third product line entered the portfolio, and the information behind the learning kept changing. I owned the learning solution: objectives and seller practice, assessment, LMS delivery, support, and later revisions. Source teams validated product truth.

What I Inherited

The business was consolidating. The learning system had to become coherent while its source material moved.

This was an informational overhaul, not a course refresh. I had to turn a changing portfolio into a seller pathway people could actually follow.

01 / Origin Two historic lines and a new third line needed one pathway.

The original lines came from separate business operations that were merging. The new line had to join the same broader portfolio without erasing meaningful distinctions for sellers.

At the same time, messaging, product features and names, terminology, target verticals, positioning, go-to-market routes, brand themes, visual design, audience strategy, assessment, and LMS delivery all needed reconsideration. The learning experience had to stay consistent while those business decisions evolved.

What I Owned

I received the learning need, introductory source material, and a timeline. I designed the learning solution around them.

Product facts and business boundaries required source-team and SME validation. My responsibility was to make that evolving information learnable, testable, deliverable, and maintainable.

Source teams + SMEsProduct truth

What made this cross-functional work different

Source teams were still partly divided by their historic operations. I reconciled multiple owners and interpretations of scope, documented who could confirm what, preserved decision history, and kept the certification internally consistent as authority and organizational hierarchy changed.

An Early Enablement Gap

I recognized a broader learner need before the original scope could fully address it.

The approved work initially sat in a training/content lane. Other departments controlled parts of the content and post-training journey, so I could not simply expand the certification into a complete enablement solution on my own. I improved the pathway, practice, terminology, and support I could control; documented the larger need; and returned to it as the organization and my work became more directly aligned with enablement.

Continuous Redesign

The certification became easier to evolve because the decisions behind it stayed visible.

Product information, learner feedback, SME conversations, sales, marketing, enablement, and changing market language gave me a broader view of where the portfolio was heading. I used that pattern recognition to keep the structure flexible. When names, messaging, audiences, features, or organizational ownership changed, the work could often be refined in bounded sections rather than rebuilt from the beginning.

I did not predict every decision. I learned to design for the direction of the business as well as the current source document, and to retain enough decision history that new reviewers could understand why the pathway was built the way it was.

What This Taught Me

Build the learning system for its next version, not only its first release.

Preserve decision history

When source owners or terminology change, documented rationale protects consistency.

Understand the business

Portfolio and market context improve learner decisions and future flexibility.

Separate authority from design

SMEs validate product truth; I design how learners understand, practice, and demonstrate it.

Interpret feedback in context

A learner signal may point to content, assessment, operations, organizational scope, or all four.

Treat the LMS as design

Access, routing, completion, reporting, and support shape the actual pathway.

Plan for change

Reusable structure reduces the cost of inevitable product, message, and audience revisions.
