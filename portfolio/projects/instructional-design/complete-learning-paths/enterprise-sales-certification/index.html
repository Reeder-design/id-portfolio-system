I rebuilt a multi-course sales certification as two business operations and product lines came together, a third product line entered the portfolio, and the information behind the learning kept changing. I owned the learning solution: objectives and seller practice, assessment, LMS delivery, support, and later revisions. Source teams validated product truth.

What I Inherited

The business was consolidating. The learning system had to become coherent while its source material moved.

This was an informational overhaul, not a course refresh. I had to turn a changing portfolio into a seller pathway people could actually follow.

01 / Origin Two historic lines and a new third line needed one pathway.

The original lines came from separate business operations that were merging. The new line had to join the same broader portfolio without erasing meaningful distinctions for sellers.

At the same time, messaging, product features and names, terminology, target verticals, positioning, go-to-market routes, brand themes, visual design, audience strategy, assessment, and LMS delivery all needed reconsideration. The learning experience had to stay consistent while those business decisions evolved.

What I Owned

I owned the learning system.

I received a learning need, early source material, and a deadline. Source teams validated product truth; I turned that evolving truth into a pathway sellers could use and administrators could run.

An Early Enablement Gap

I could see the enablement gap before I could bridge it.

The approved scope began with training content. I improved the practice, terminology, and support within my control, documented the broader need, and returned to it when the organization and my work connected more directly with enablement.

Original scopeTrainingClarity · practice · resources
Post-training applicationRole supportCross-team ownership
Broader needEnablementSupport beyond completion

I could not unilaterally expand another team’s scope. Keeping the gap visible helped me make bounded improvements first, then act on the larger opportunity when ownership aligned.

Continuous Redesign

Visible decisions made the certification easier to evolve.

Product updates, feedback, SME conversations, sales, marketing, and enablement revealed where the portfolio was heading. I kept the structure flexible so names, messages, audiences, and features could be revised in bounded sections.

I did not predict every change. I preserved the reasoning behind each decision so new reviewers could revise the pathway without starting over.
