Interactive Learning

Interactive learning turns information into something learners can try. I use realistic decisions, practice screens, revealing visuals, and responsive feedback so people can apply an idea, see what happens, and try again.

01

Real-world conversation practice

Practice scenarios

Learners step into a realistic conversation or decision process, choose what to say or do, and see how the next moment changes. A branching customer conversation or escalation can make the consequences of a choice safe to examine and retry.

Use for: customer conversations, coaching moments, service recovery, and decisions under changing conditions.

02

Evidence of understanding

Assessments

Interactive assessments can ask learners to classify evidence, apply criteria, sequence steps, or explain a decision. Specific feedback reveals misconceptions early and shows both the learner and designer what needs more practice.

Use for: formative checks, applied scoring, retrieval practice, and confident skill demonstration.

03

Audio, video, and active viewing

Interactive media

Voice overs, interactive video, audio cues, and timed prompts help learners listen and look with purpose. They can pause, replay, inspect a moment, or make a choice before the media continues.

Use for: narrated demonstrations, observed behaviors, visual cues, and decisions embedded in media.

04

Guided platform practice

UI demos & simulations

Step by step UI walkthroughs and simulations let learners rehearse the screens, workflows, and platforms they use. A guided sequence makes the next action visible before they work in a live system.

Use for: product ordering, sales workflows, software navigation, and platform handoffs.

05

Explore, sort, and connect

Interactive graphics & text

Hotspots, click-to-reveal, drag and drop, matching, and expandable text invite learners to work with the information. These interactions can uncover detail in a graphic or help them organize relationships themselves.

Use for: process maps, product visuals, evidence matching, and layered explanations.

06

Progressive skill practice

Gamification

Points, milestones, and badges can give repeated practice a clear arc. I use them only when they support a meaningful goal, so the reward reinforces useful performance instead of replacing it.

Best fit: recurring practice, staged skill-building, and low-stakes challenges.
