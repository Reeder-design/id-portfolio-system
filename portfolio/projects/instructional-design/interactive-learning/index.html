Interactive Learning

Interactive learning turns information into something learners can try. I use realistic decisions, practice screens, revealing visuals, and responsive feedback so people can apply an idea, see what happens, and try again.

Interaction design methodology

Choose the interaction by what learners need to do.

Each format creates a different kind of practice. I choose it by the decision, action, or feedback the work calls for, then build the interaction around that purpose.

01

Scenarios and branching simulations

Practice a decision before it counts.

Realistic choices let learners test a judgment and see how a different move changes the outcome. I use this pattern when the challenge is applying criteria in a situation, not recalling a definition.

Best fit: sales conversations, qualification, escalation, or other consequence-driven decisions.

02

Quizzes and knowledge checks

Check the distinction that matters.

Matching, labeling, and short questions make a learner retrieve and apply a concept. I use them to expose a misconception early and give a specific explanation rather than a generic right-or-wrong message.

Best fit: terminology, classification, process steps, and knowledge that must be recognized quickly.

03

Interactive videos

Turn watching into noticing.

A pause, hotspot, or embedded question can ask learners to interpret a moment before the video moves on. I use this when seeing a demonstration matters, but passive viewing would hide the decision behind the action.

Best fit: demonstrations, conversations, safety cues, and observable behaviors.

04

Software and system simulations

Practice the workflow safely.

A simulated interface lets learners try the same sequence they will perform at work, with cues where mistakes are costly. I use this when knowing the screen and the order of actions matter as much as understanding the concept.

Best fit: LMS tasks, reporting workflows, system handoffs, and admin procedures.

05

Hotspots and click-to-reveal graphics

Explore a visual at the right depth.

Instead of crowding a diagram with labels, I let learners reveal details in context. This is useful when relationships or parts are easier to understand spatially than in a long explanation.

Best fit: product anatomy, process maps, data views, and annotated examples.

06

Gamified challenges

Make progress visible.

Points, milestones, and badges can give repeated practice a clear arc. I use them only when they support a meaningful goal, so the reward reinforces useful performance instead of replacing it.

Best fit: recurring practice, staged skill-building, and low-stakes challenges.

07

Learner-facing AI

Make practice fit the person.

An AI-supported experience can adapt a scenario, offer a relevant explanation, or respond to an open question in the moment. I use this design direction when the learner benefits from individualized context or feedback that a small set of scripted paths cannot provide.

Best fit: roleplay, tailored practice paths, tutoring, and deeper feedback on learner responses.
