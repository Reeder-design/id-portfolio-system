Design Approach

Interaction should give the learner something meaningful to practice.

I don't add interaction just to make content clickable. I use it when the learner benefits from making a decision, interpreting information, testing an assumption, practicing a process, or seeing the consequences of a choice.

That makes scenario-based learning especially useful for sales enablement, qualification, customer discovery, assessment, and other situations where knowing the information is different from being able to use it.

What I Design For

Practice before performance.

  • Recognizing relevant evidence
  • Making decisions with incomplete information
  • Applying frameworks to realistic situations
  • Receiving specific corrective feedback
  • Comparing learner judgment with an expert model
  • Connecting content to the next real-world action

Behind the Interaction

The interaction follows the learning decision.

I start with what the learner should be able to recognize, decide, or do. The interaction pattern comes after that.

1

Give the Learner Context

Provide enough customer, product, scenario, or performance information for the learner to make a meaningful decision.

2

Ask for Judgment

Move beyond recognition or recall by asking the learner to interpret evidence, classify information, prioritize, score, or choose what should happen next.

3

Respond to the Decision

Feedback should reference the information the learner actually used and explain why the decision is strong, incomplete, or unsupported.

4

Connect It to Performance

End with the implication of the decision: what should the seller, employee, manager, or learner actually do next?

Custom Development

Built as reusable web interactions.

These examples are built with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript so I can experiment with interaction logic outside a traditional authoring tool and better understand how the underlying components work.

The longer-term goal is to separate scenario data from interaction logic so patterns like scoring, classification, feedback, dashboards, and decision models can be reused across future learning projects.
