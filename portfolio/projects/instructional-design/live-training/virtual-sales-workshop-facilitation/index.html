01

Need

What needed to change?

Help experienced sellers work through a shared sales-qualification framework in a live virtual setting without reducing the session to slide narration or one-way explanation.

Audience

Experienced enterprise sellers participating in a multi-session virtual sales workshop.

What learners needed to do

  • Connect qualification concepts to realistic seller situations and customer evidence.
  • Use discussion and polling to distinguish stronger and weaker interpretations.
  • Explain reasoning through seller examples rather than terminology alone.
  • Surface questions and friction that need clarification during or after the session.
02

Design Decisions

How I shaped the solution

Approach

Use the prepared facilitator and participant materials as the backbone, but treat live time as a place for explanation, discussion, polling, examples, and application. Keep the objective fixed while adjusting pacing and explanation to the audience response.

05

Facilitation Moves

Use the room as part of the learning design.

Participation gives me evidence about what needs more explanation, less explanation, or a different example.

Diagnose

Ask before telling

Use questions to surface the learner's current interpretation before adding more explanation.

Check

Poll with a purpose

Use quick polls to check confidence or interpretation, then change the debrief based on the response.

Apply

Use seller examples

Invite relevant examples from experienced learners so abstract concepts stay connected to actual sales work.

Adapt

Reframe when needed

Change the explanation, example, or pacing when the first version does not land cleanly.

06

Real-Time Adaptation

Read signals instead of following the deck mechanically.

Live facilitation requires decisions about what to expand, compress, repeat, or hand off.

SignalWhat it suggestsFacilitation response
Low confidence in a pollThe concept or distinction is not stable yet.Slow down, reframe, and test again with an example.
The same question keeps returningThe current explanation is not resolving the friction.Use a different explanation or connect it to a concrete seller situation.
Learners offer strong relevant examplesThe group already has usable experience to build from.Use peer examples and compress lower-value background.
Time is tighteningThe agenda needs a tradeoff rather than a rushed ending.Protect application and debrief time; shorten lower-value narration.
07

Design Feedback Loop

Facilitation makes me a better instructional designer.

Live questions reveal where future self-paced or facilitator-led learning needs better support.

Clarify

Recurring questions

Show where future content needs clearer explanation, examples, or reference support.

Practice

Common misconceptions

Become candidates for scenario distractors, feedback, and formative checks.

Ground

Strong learner examples

Reveal the kinds of realistic situations that make later learning feel more credible.

Refine

Timing friction

Shows which explanations or activities need to be simplified before another facilitator delivers the session.

08

Evidence

Keep facilitation claims tied to what is actually supported.

This case demonstrates delivery and design judgment without inventing learner-performance results.

Delivered

Supported

Assigned modules were facilitated in a multi-session virtual sales workshop using discussion, polls, seller examples, and real-time pacing adjustments.

Observed

Supported

Recurring questions were captured as useful feedback for future learning design and facilitator support.

Boundary

Not claimed

No learner-score improvement, sales-performance lift, revenue impact, or workshop satisfaction metric is claimed without approved evidence.

09

Outcome

What came out of the work?

  • Facilitated assigned modules within a larger multi-session sales workshop for experienced sellers.
  • Used discussion, polling, and seller examples to create participation instead of relying on presentation alone.
  • Adjusted pacing and explanations based on learner response while keeping the session aligned to its objectives.
  • Turned recurring learner questions and friction into useful input for future content, scenarios, and facilitator guidance.
