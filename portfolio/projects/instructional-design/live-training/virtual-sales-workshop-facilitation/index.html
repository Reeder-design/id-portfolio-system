Workshop in Practice

Structure before the session. Judgment during it.

MEDDPICC is a sales qualification framework. The shared goal was to help sellers assess opportunity evidence, see what was missing, and choose a useful next discovery action. Participants brought different levels of company, market, and enterprise-sales context.

Prepared foundation

Facilitator + participant materials

I prepared the objective, activity sequence, timing, transitions, prompts, and debrief for my assigned modules from the existing workshop materials.

Live judgment

Respond to what learners show

I used discussion, polls, questions, and seller examples to hear the reasoning behind answers, then adjusted explanation, examples, or pace while keeping the objective fixed.

Explore the five-stage arc to see what each activity made visible and what I did next.

Prepare

Plan the route through the assigned module.

The objective and existing materials set the destination. I prepared how to move between explanation, participation, application, and debrief.

My move

Map the timing, transitions, prompts, and a realistic way to ask for evidence.

What I watch for

Where learners may need more context, and where a question or example can expose their reasoning.

Next decision

Open with the seller task, then invite an interpretation before adding more detail.

Objective Sequence Prompt
See a public-safe practice example Fictional discovery note

Discovery note: The buyer describes a procurement review after vendor selection, but the seller has not identified who approves the budget.

Ask: Which MEDDPICC category has the strongest evidence here? What should the seller ask next?

Reasoning: The procurement review primarily supports Paper Process. The missing budget approver points to an Economic Buyer gap. A useful next question is who has financial decision authority and when that person joins the process.

Design Feedback

The live session also informed what future support should clarify.

After delivery, I treated repeated friction as input for clearer learning content, scenarios, and facilitator guidance.

Recurring questions

Point to explanations or reference support that need to be easier to find and use.

Common misconceptions

Suggest scenario choices and formative feedback that make the distinction more visible.

Strong seller examples

Reveal realistic situations that can make future practice credible across audiences.

Timing friction

Shows where to simplify background so application and debrief keep their place.

Observe → interpret → adjust → check again → carry the pattern into future design
