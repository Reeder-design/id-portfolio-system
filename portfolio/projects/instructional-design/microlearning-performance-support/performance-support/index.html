Point-of-need design

Performance Support

I use performance support when work is happening and the next action depends on a reliable answer. I trace the friction, check the source with the right owner, and put guidance where a person will actually use it.

The strategy helps someone find, interpret, apply, or act at the moment of need. A guide, response template, dashboard, or job aid is one possible output.

Performance consulting lens

Look through the problem before choosing the support.

I examine what people know, what they can reach, how the work flows, which information they trust, what the system allows, and the feedback they receive. That analysis reveals where support or a change to the work itself could improve performance.

Knowledge and skill

When a task depends on a concept or judgment, I look for the step a person cannot yet explain or perform independently.

Observe
Repeated questions or inconsistent decisions across similar tasks.
Test
Can the person apply the rule to a new example, or do they only need a reminder?
Improve
Build practice for new judgment and a quick reference for stable steps.
POSSIBLE SUPPORT MOVEPractice + job aid

Teach the decision, then put a concise cue beside the real task.

Purpose before format

Different forms of L&D performance support.

Before choosing a material, I look at the task, the point of friction, the source of the answer, and what successful performance looks like. The support strategy may use one of these formats, combine several, or reveal a workflow change that a material alone cannot solve.

01 / DO THE TASK

Job aids and quick-reference guides

A checklist, cheat sheet, or workflow map keeps a multi-step task visible while someone performs it. The useful version shows the order, critical checks, and where to go for full detail without making the worker open a course.

Use when
A procedure is detailed, infrequent, or easy to miss under pressure.
Design focus
Sequence the steps, mark decision points, and link to the maintained source.
02 / CHOOSE THE PATH

Decision guides and flowcharts

A logic tree or decision table turns complex rules into questions a worker can answer in real time. It helps them choose the next action, troubleshoot a branch, and see when a human decision is needed.

Use when
The right action changes with conditions or inputs.
Design focus
Make the rule, branch labels, and escalation threshold unambiguous.
03 / FIND THE ANSWER

Searchable knowledge bases and glossaries

A searchable repository or term list gives people a reliable place to check definitions, policies, and product information. Search terms, result summaries, and clear ownership matter as much as the content itself.

Use when
Many answers must stay current and be found quickly.
Design focus
Use the worker’s language, surface the best answer, and show who maintains it.
04 / REFRESH ONE IDEA

Microlearning assets

A focused video, audio clip, or infographic can refresh one concept right before it is needed. The asset works best when it is easy to reopen and points back to a usable reference for the live task.

Use when
A brief explanation or demonstration will restore understanding.
Design focus
Keep one objective, show the action, and make the next step easy to find.
Explore the separate Microlearning page →
05 / HELP IN THE WORKFLOW

Embedded context-sensitive guidance

Tooltips, in-app help, and source-backed assistants can answer a question without pulling someone out of the software they are using. The cue needs to match the field or step, point to trusted information, and leave a clear route for a case it cannot answer.

Use when
The problem appears at a specific point inside a digital workflow.
Design focus
Trigger help at the right moment and keep its source and owner visible.
06 / PRODUCE AN OUTPUT

Interactive playbooks and worksheets

A playbook or worksheet pairs prompts with a worked example and space to build an output. It can support a client call, review, or planning task while preserving room for judgment rather than scripting every word.

Use when
Someone must create an artifact or guide a conversation.
Design focus
Show the sequence, model a strong response, and capture the next action.

These visuals illustrate public-safe design patterns. They do not show private learner records, support threads, or employer systems.
