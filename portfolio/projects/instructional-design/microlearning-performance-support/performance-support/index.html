Point-of-need design

Performance Support

Performance support is how I address the part of a work problem that training cannot solve by itself. When someone needs an answer during a task, I look for the real source of friction, work with the people who own the answer, and place trusted guidance where the work happens.

The goal is to help someone find, interpret, apply, and act on the right information at the moment of need. A guide, template, dashboard, or job aid is a possible format; the strategy starts with the task.

Need appears →Find + interpret →Act or escalate

The resource stays available for the next real case.

Performance consulting lens

Start with the friction, not a deliverable.

Recurring learner and operational questions can sound like a request for more training. Working with them taught me to ask what is actually blocking the person: understanding, access, a process, source ownership, configuration, or an unusual case. The answer may be learning, support, a workflow change, a system fix, human escalation, or a combination.

Need to learn?Build a mental model, judgment, or a new skill.Learning
Need to find or use?Get a reliable answer, procedure, source, or next step during work.Performance support

Team strategy

A useful answer needs an operational home.

Performance support is often cross-functional. I work with the appropriate learning, system, operations, administration, or subject-matter owners to validate the guidance and decide who can resolve exceptions.

Public-safe work patterns

Match the support pattern to the problem.

My work includes reusable templates, access guidance, issue categories, source and incoming-information guides, change workflows, and maintained operational references. These examples show the logic with fictionalized details, not private cases.

Issue-to-response supportWhen recurring questions need consistent triage

For questions about a missing certification, transcript, access, enrollment, or migrated history, identify the issue category, check the authoritative source, give the applicable response, and route exceptions to the right owner.

Issue→Source check→Response→Escalate if needed
Operational referenceWhen teams need a maintained answer path

A reference can map each request to the information needed, a reusable response, and an escalation route. That makes source consistency and handoffs visible without treating every case as self-service.

Request→Needed info→Response→Escalation route
Connected learning supportWhen a lesson needs a live-work companion

A launch experience can teach the idea and let someone practice. A linked, maintained resource then supplies the current answer when the real task occurs. I decide what belongs in the lesson and what will change too often to embed there.

Learn→Practice→Use→Return

Purpose before format: quick action may call for a decision prompt or reusable response; current status for a reporting view; deeper detail for a guide or source system; a conversation for a talk track or deck; and ongoing reinforcement for connected program support.

What the work taught me

Design learning for what happens after completion.

Support work changed the questions I ask while building formal learning: What principle needs teaching? What needs practice? What changes often? Where will someone look during the task? Who maintains that answer, and what happens when it is not enough?

When the same issue keeps appearing, I treat it as evidence to revisit the resource, documentation, configuration, QA, or workflow with the right owner.
