The Need

Get sellers ready for launch without asking them to sit through a full certification course.

I needed to translate approved launch material into something sellers could actually use before customer conversations. They needed enough context to explain the value, recognize likely fit, and know the next step, but they did not need to memorize every product detail.

Keep it focused

I scoped the experience around customer problem, value, fit, and action rather than reproducing the source material.

Make judgment visible

I separated practice so sellers had to choose a response instead of only reviewing launch messaging.

Plan for change

I kept the assets modular because launch guidance can change faster than a larger curriculum.

Review + Refinement

I used review comments to improve the experience, not just correct the copy.

Instructions

I tightened directions where the learner action was not immediately obvious.

Feedback

I refined scenario feedback so it explained the reasoning behind the choice instead of only marking it right or wrong.

Navigation + completion

I revised readability, navigation, and completion messaging so the experience ended with a clear next step.

Outcome

The finished launch experience paired quick context with deliberate practice.

Learn, then practice

I delivered a paired experience instead of an oversized launch course.

Decision-based practice

The Storyline scenario moved the learner beyond passive product review into a customer-facing choice.

Modular updates

The two assets could be revised independently as positioning or launch guidance changed.

Public-safe case study: product names, proprietary launch messaging, internal screenshots, customer details, reviewer comments, and source materials are omitted.
