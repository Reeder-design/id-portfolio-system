Vertical Positioning Microlearning

I designed and developed one Rise microlearning module for internal sellers and channel partners, using a shared learning architecture to teach two different industry contexts: seaport and airport.

Design Need

Teach two vertical contexts without building two courses or flattening them into one generic story.

Seaport and airport environments needed different customer priorities, examples, and practice. I kept them distinct inside one short module while giving learners a consistent structure they did not have to relearn.

Enough context

Give sellers the operating context needed to make positioning and use cases credible.

Real application

Move beyond industry facts by asking the learner to make a customer-facing decision.

Controlled scope

Keep deeper technical and reference material available without putting all of it inside the microlearning.

Interactive Learning Architecture

Switch industries, then walk the learning pattern.

The architecture stays consistent so learners know what to expect. The customer context, use cases, and judgment change by vertical.

Seaport component

Keep the seller grounded in a complex, distributed operating environment.

The seaport section framed the environment first, then connected that context to sales-relevant use cases and a customer-facing decision.

Rise 360 pattern

Orient to the operating environment.

I opened with just enough context for sellers to understand why connectivity, mobility, and operational visibility matter in a distributed seaport setting.

Learning evidenceOperating context + customer priorities

The goal was fluency, not a technical deep dive.

Design decision

I narrowed the source material to the vertical context sellers actually needed before a discovery or positioning conversation.

What it produced

A concise content set for seaport and airport rather than a broad industry course.

Media + Review

I used multimedia where it helped orientation and review where accuracy mattered.

The module did not need to become a video course. Media supported the learning pattern; specialist review protected the vertical-specific content.

Colossyan orientation

I used a short AI-presenter segment where narrated context helped introduce the environment efficiently.

Selective multimedia

Rise + scenario practice

The core experience stayed scannable and interactive, with customer-facing decisions rather than passive content alone.

Application over recall

Review 360 + Asana

I coordinated QA and specialist feedback, then tracked revisions to wording, scope, examples, and media choices through the review process.

Traceable review

Design Outcome

One concise learning system that kept two verticals distinct.

No invented performance metrics here. The case study focuses on the observable design result and the structure I built.

Focused

Vertical context without a full vertical curriculum.

The module gave sellers enough operating context to make the use cases meaningful, then stopped before the experience became an encyclopedic industry course.

Design evidenceOne concise Rise module with two distinct components

Public-safe case study: proprietary vertical messaging, internal screenshots, customer details, reviewer comments, and source material are intentionally omitted.
