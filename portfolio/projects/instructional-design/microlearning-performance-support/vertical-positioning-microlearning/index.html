Vertical-Positioning Microlearning

I designed and developed two short sales-learning modules for different industry settings after realizing the original combined scope was too broad for a useful microlearning experience. Each module gave sellers enough operating context to recognize customer needs, connect them to use cases, practice a decision, and find deeper resources.

The Need

Give sellers usable industry context without building a broad vertical course.

I was working with a topic that originally combined multiple industry environments. As the content developed, I saw that the scope was fighting the microlearning goal. Instead of forcing everything into one lesson, I split the work into two focused modules.

Context mattered

Sellers needed enough industry context to understand the operating environment before the use cases would feel credible.

Time mattered

The lesson had to stay short enough to use before discovery or positioning conversations.

Depth had to live elsewhere

I used follow-up resources for details that did not belong inside the short learner path.

Scoping Decision

I split one broad topic into two focused modules.

The original combined scope became too broad for a true microlearning experience, so I separated the industry contexts instead of squeezing both into one lesson.

Problem

The combined topic created too much context switching and too much content for the moment of need.

Decision

Separate seaport and airport learning while keeping a common design pattern.

Result

Shorter learner paths with clearer industry boundaries.

My decision

I reviewed the original combined scope and identified where two industry contexts were competing for limited learner attention.

What it produced

A decision to split the work into separate seaport and airport modules.

Media + Review

I used specialist review and scoped multimedia to make the short format credible.

Short presenter segment

I used Colossyan for a concise introduction where a presenter added orientation without taking over the module.

Scenario practice

I kept application inside the learner flow so the module still required a customer-facing decision.

Specialist review

I coordinated internal QA and subject-matter review for each industry area and revised content where the short format needed more precision.

Outcome

The finished work became two focused modules instead of one diluted course.

Clearer boundaries

I turned one broad topic into two shorter learner paths with distinct operating contexts.

Repeatable structure

Both modules use the same context, use cases, practice, and resources pattern without pretending the industries are interchangeable.

Useful depth

Specialist review and selective multimedia added credibility without pushing the lessons beyond the intended microlearning scope.

Public-safe case study: proprietary industry messaging, customer details, internal screenshots, reviewer comments, and source material are omitted.
