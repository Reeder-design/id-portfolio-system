Overview

Product knowledge is only the starting point.

A seller can know every feature of a product and still struggle to identify the right opportunity, uncover meaningful customer pain, position value, or move a pursuit forward.

That's why I think about sales enablement as more than transferring information. The learning has to help someone use that information during an actual customer conversation or pursuit.

Enablement Question: What does the seller need to recognize, decide, say, or do differently when they're back in front of a customer?

Design Approach

What I optimize for.

Customer First

Lead with the customer's environment, problem, and desired outcome rather than starting with a feature dump.

Practice Decisions

Sellers should practice choosing what to ask, what to prioritize, and how to respond to realistic pursuit information.

Keep Technical Depth Useful

Include enough technical context to support credibility and qualification without turning sales training into engineering training.

Build for Scale

Use consistent frameworks, templates, certification paths, and partner-ready resources that can be reused across audiences.

Reflection

What good sales enablement looks like to me.

The strongest sales learning doesn't end when someone can describe a product.

It gives sellers a better way to recognize customer needs, evaluate opportunities, qualify what they know and don't know, connect capabilities to outcomes, and decide what needs to happen next.

That's also why I like scenario-based learning so much for sales audiences: the real skill is usually judgment.
