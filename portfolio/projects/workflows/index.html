How I Think About Workflow

Good processes make good work easier to repeat.

A strong workflow reduces unnecessary friction, creates clearer handoffs, makes quality easier to maintain, and helps teams reuse what already works.

In L&D, that can mean anything from documenting a design process to automating repetitive reporting, creating reusable development components, or using AI within a structured review loop.

I think of workflows as part of the learning solution itself because the way the work is designed, developed, deployed, tracked, and improved directly affects the final learner experience.

Connected Workflow

The pieces aren't really separate.

Design, development, AI, data, and reporting often overlap. A complete learning workflow can connect all of them.

1

Identify the Need

Define the business problem, audience, performance gap, constraints, and expected learning or performance outcome.

2

Design the Solution

Create the learning strategy, architecture, objectives, practice, assessment plan, and delivery approach.

3

Develop + Review

Build the learning experience using reusable components and structured review, with AI supporting selected tasks where useful.

4

Deploy + Track

Publish through the learning ecosystem, manage enrollment or certification logic, and collect completion and assessment data.

5

Analyze + Report

Clean and validate the data, produce reporting outputs, identify patterns, and surface information stakeholders can actually use.

6

Feed It Back

Use learner data, stakeholder feedback, QA findings, and business outcomes to improve the next version of the solution.
